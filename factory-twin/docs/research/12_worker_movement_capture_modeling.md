## 1. Camera placement for floor-plane tracking

### Geometry you can compute before going on site
For a pinhole camera at height *h*, tilt *t* below horizontal, vertical FOV *V* and horizontal FOV *H*:

- Near edge of ground coverage: `d_near = h / tan(t + V/2)`; far edge: `d_far = h / tan(t − V/2)` (infinite if `t ≤ V/2`). Horizontal coverage width at ground distance *d*: `w = 2·d·tan(H/2)`. Pixel density `px/m = W_px / w`. (Same formulas as the planning calculators at [diysmarthomehub](https://www.diysmarthomehub.com/?p=19213) and [cctvdesigntool](https://cctvdesigntool.com/calculators/fov); Irisity's analytics placement guide says angled mounts are typically tilted 10–40° — [docs.irisity.com](https://docs.irisity.com/iris-plus/camera-placement-principles).)

**[est.] Worked numbers for a 4K (3840 px wide) camera**, 2.8 mm lens (≈101°×55°, the Reolink RLC-810A 2.8 mm variant spec — [spec sheet](https://www.robbshop.nl/media/30/0e/b1/1660755986/REO_RLC_810A_download_1.pdf)) or 4 mm lens (87°×44°, [CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2020/11/17/reolink-rlc-810a-smart-4k-poe-ip-camera-specifications-and-u...)):

| h (m) | tilt | lens | ground near–far (m) | px/m at 10 / 20 / 30 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 35° | 2.8 mm | 2.1 – 30 | 158 / 79 / 53 |
| 4 | 25° | 4 mm | 3.7 – 76 | 202 / 101 / 67 |
| 6 | 45° | 2.8 mm | 1.9 – 19 | 158 / 79 / 53 |
| 6 | 30° | 4 mm | 4.7 – 43 | 202 / 101 / 67 |
| 8 | 60° | 2.8 mm | 0.3 – 12.6 | — |

Rule of thumb: a 1.7 m person at 30 m from a 2.8 mm 4K camera is ~90 px tall (fine for YOLO person detection; 1080p halves this to ~45 px, the lower practical limit). Surveillance planning figures of ~60 px/ft for face detail and ~20 px/ft for scene overview are quoted by installers ([diysmarthomehub](https://www.diysmarthomehub.com/?p=19213)) — tracking needs the "overview" tier, not the face tier.

### Overhead vs oblique
- **Overhead/ceiling (near-nadir) wide-angle** removes most occlusion by 2–3 m machines and makes foot localisation nearly distance-independent, but each camera covers little floor (at 6 m, roughly 10×14 m usable [est.]) and edge distortion is severe. Mori et al. (Nagoya Univ., 2025) tracked workers with **19 ceiling wide-angle cameras** in a logistics warehouse, found the distortion is strongest vertically near image edges, and report **>20 % tracking-accuracy gain** from aligning detections across cameras by **foot position instead of full bounding boxes** ([arXiv 2510.19432](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19432v1)). A companion paper notes bounding-box-derived locations "often carry errors" under wide-angle distortion ([arXiv 2510.26369](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26369v1)).
- **Flanders Make (Logistics 2025)** used **6 ceiling cameras with intersecting FOVs**, a placement-optimisation tool to verify coverage before deployment, YOLO detection of pallets/boxes/forklifts, and report **≈84 % detection+tracking accuracy under occlusion/overlap**, feeding a digital twin ([MDPI Logistics 9(4):153](https://www.mdpi.com/2305-6290/9/4/153); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396259343_Real-Time_Warehouse_Monitoring_with_Ceiling_Cameras_and_Digital_Twin_for_Asset_Tracking_and_Scene_Analysis)). Check the full text for mounting height; the summaries I obtained did not state it.
- **Oblique (25–45° tilt, 4–6 m high)** is what three cameras force you into for 1,000–3,000 m²: each covers an aisle corridor 20–40 m long, but anything behind a 2–3 m machine is lost. The CVPRW 2023 AI City paper identifies occlusion as *the* main failure mode of indoor multi-camera people tracking because it changes appearance and breaks ground-plane localisation ([Kim et al., CVPRW 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023W/AICity/papers/Kim_Addressing_the_Occlusion_Problem_in_Multi-Camera_People_Tracking_With_Human_CVPRW_2023_paper.pdf)).

**How many cameras [est.]:** with oblique 4K cameras at 5–6 m the reliable tracking footprint per camera is ~300–500 m² (a 12–15 m wide × 25–35 m long aisle corridor; beyond ~30 m foot-point error exceeds 0.3 m, see §2). So 1,000 m² needs 3–4 cameras, 3,000 m² needs 8–10, and tall machines add 1–2 per blocked aisle. With only **3 cameras** you cannot cover a 3,000 m² hall; cover the **main material-flow spine** (the aisles where carts and the forklift actually travel) and accept gaps at workstations, where dwell can be inferred from entry/exit.

### Practical hardware/recording
- **Reolink RLC-810A** (8 MP, PoE, RTSP, 87° or 101° HFOV depending on lens) records 24/7 to NVR or microSD and streams RTSP ([smarthomecompared](https://smarthomecompared.com/cameras/reolink-rlc-810a-vs-tp-link-tapo-c500)). **Tapo** cameras support RTSP/ONVIF but TP-Link states that SD-card, cloud and NVR/ONVIF cannot all be used simultaneously ("choose two") because of limited hardware ([Tapo FAQ](https://test.tapo.com/th/faq/713/)) — fine if you only use NVR/RTSP recording. Old phones: workable for a 1-day pilot, not for 14 days (thermal throttling, storage, no PoE).
- Storage **[est.]**: 1080p H.265 ≈ 2 Mbps ≈ 21 GB/day/camera; 4K H.265 ≈ 6 Mbps ≈ 63 GB/day. 3 cameras × 14 days × 10 h ≈ 0.4 TB (1080p) or 1.1 TB (4K). Record at native 15–25 fps; process at 5–10 fps.
- **Lighting**: 50/60 Hz fluorescent and PWM-driven LED lighting produce rolling-shutter banding; set the camera's anti-flicker to the mains frequency, and note that LED PWM drivers can flicker at non-mains frequencies up to ~2 kHz, which a 50/60 Hz setting will not fix ([patent US9865044](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9865044); [ams sensor note](https://www.mbtmag.com/home/sensors/product/21128631/ams-highsensitivity-optical-sensor)). **Dust** (wood dust is the furniture-factory specific issue): use IP66/67 bullet housings, wipe lenses at least weekly, and recalibrate the homography if a camera is touched (I found no published study on dust effects; treat as practice).

### Wide-angle distortion
Calibrate each lens with a chessboard using OpenCV's `cv::fisheye` model for ≥100° lenses (the pinhole+radial model under-fits) and undistort *detection points* (not the whole frame) before applying the homography ([OpenCV fisheye docs](https://docs.opencv.org/5.x/d2/d14/namespacecv_1_1fisheye.html); forum discussion of high RMS when the wrong model is used: [forum.opencv.org](https://forum.opencv.org/t/wide-angle-or-fisheye-lens-the-undistorted-result-and-how-to-fix-the-perspective-distortion/5440)). For very wide lenses, full-frame undistortion introduces resampling distortion; mapping points directly is cleaner ([arXiv 1707.03775](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.03775)).

## 2. Image-to-floor mapping accuracy

### Homography from control points
With a flat floor, image point (u,v) → floor (X,Y) via a 3×3 homography **H** estimated from ≥4 non-collinear floor points (use 8–12 spread over the whole usable area and solve with RANSAC/LM; check reprojection RMS in metres by holding out points). The WILDTRACK multi-camera benchmark (7 cameras, 12×36 m) quantises the ground plane to 2.5 cm cells and evaluates with a **0.5 m matching radius**; top methods reach MODA ≈ 91–96 on WILDTRACK/MultiviewX ([WILDTRACK](https://fleuret.org/papers/chavdarova-et-al-cvpr2018.pdf); [MVDet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.07247); [TrackTacular](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.12573)). So "well-calibrated multi-camera, ≤0.5 m" is the published norm; sub-0.5 m errors are rarely reported.

### Error vs distance — the governing formula
Szulc & Iwanowski (2026) derive analytically that homography-based ground-plane distance error **grows roughly quadratically with distance from the camera** and propose regression or gradient-descent re-optimisation of H to correct it (>19 M simulated samples) ([arXiv 2604.10805](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10805)). The quadratic law follows from the pinhole geometry: for a camera at height *h* with focal length *f_px*, a vertical pixel error *e* at ground distance *d* produces a range error

`ΔX ≈ e · (d² + h²) / (h · f_px)`

**[est.] 4K, 101° lens (f_px ≈ 1583), per 1 px of foot-point error:** h = 4 m → 0.018 / 0.066 / 0.145 m at d = 10 / 20 / 30 m; h = 6 m → 0.014 / 0.046 / 0.099 m. At **1080p** the per-pixel error doubles (0.037 / 0.131 / 0.289 m at h = 4 m). Real foot-point jitter is 3–8 px, so **expect ≈0.1 m at 10 m, 0.3–0.5 m at 20 m, 0.6–1.2 m at 30 m** for a single oblique 4K camera at 4–6 m **[est.]**, consistent with the roadside study below. Raising the camera and keeping the usable range ≤ 25 m is the cheapest accuracy lever.

### Bounding-box bottom-centre bias
- MOTChallenge notes the box bottom-centre is "likely shifted away from the true feet position" and that far pedestrians have noisier positions; even small 2D misplacements cause large 3D errors ([MOT15 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.01942)).
- A 2026 roadside-camera study: replacing the naive box centre with a ground-contact quadrilateral centre cut **median ground error from 5.5 m → 0.9 m (medium range) and 8.7 m → 1.8 m (far range)** for vehicles; normalising pixel error by box height cut mean error 0.405 → 0.170 ([arXiv 2608.05840](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05840)). Forklifts are vehicle-like: use the base-quadrilateral or a keypoint on the wheel contact, not the box bottom.
- A fisheye intersection study reports **pedestrian ground error < 0.25 m at all distances within 25 m** (0.35 m at 10 m/80° bearing) when projecting a 3D box through the lens model ([arXiv 2604.17046](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.17046)).
- **Occluded legs are the dominant bias in a factory**: if a 2–3 m machine or a cart hides the legs and the box bottom sits at height *z*, the ground error is `ΔX = d·z/(h−z)` — **[est.]** for h = 6 m, z = 0.6 m: 1.1 / 2.2 / 3.3 m at 10 / 20 / 30 m. This single effect is larger than every calibration error combined, which is the argument for higher mounts and for pose keypoints.

### Improvements
- **Ankle keypoints + homography**: a Research Square preprint reports **0.159 m mean error vs 0.386 m for a YOLOv8 box baseline** on a fixed CCTV camera (non-peer-reviewed, single scene) ([Research Square](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10698990/latest)); the "Generalizable Multi-Camera 3D Pedestrian Detection" method uses the lower ankle keypoints, corrected by the box bottom, as the ground point ([arXiv 2104.05813](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.05813)). A pose-based social-distancing pipeline is another worked example ([Springer MVA 2022 / PMC9734371](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9734371/)). When ankles are not visible, fall back to hip keypoints projected down by a nominal 0.9 m using the camera's known height (requires full extrinsics, not just H).
- **Multi-camera fusion** on the ground plane (average or Kalman-fuse the per-camera floor points weighted by `1/ΔX²`) — the NVIDIA DeepStream MV3DT sample does exactly this with a 3×4 projection matrix per camera and a ground-plane assumption ([NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/?p=119991); [deepstream_reference_apps](https://github.com/NVIDIA-AI-IOT/deepstream_reference_apps)).
- Smooth with an RTS smoother or Savitzky–Golay (offline, so non-causal smoothing is allowed) before computing speeds.

## 3. Tracking people, carts and forklifts

### Trackers and what to expect
Published pedestrian benchmarks (MOT17 test): **ByteTrack 80.3 MOTA / 77.3 IDF1 / 63.1 HOTA at ~30 fps on a V100**; MOT20 77.8 / 75.2 / 61.3 ([ByteTrack README](https://github.com/ifzhang/ByteTrack)); **BoT-SORT-ReID 80.5 / 80.2 / 65.0** ([arXiv 2206.14651](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.14651)). The boxmot repository's current table (MOT17 ablation half, same YOLOX detector) ranks: OccluBoost HOTA 71.1 > BoT-SORT 69.7 > BoostTrack 69.3 > StrongSORT 68.1 > DeepOC-SORT 68.0 > ByteTrack 67.7 > HybridSORT 67.3 > OC-SORT 66.4 ([boxmot README](https://github.com/mikel-brostrom/boxmot)). The differences are mostly **IDF1/association**, which is what matters for trip counting.

The closest indoor analogue, IndoorCrowd (multi-scene indoor), shows the drop to expect off-benchmark: **BoT-SORT 55.5 MOTA / 71.6 IDF1 vs ByteTrack 51.4 / 69.3** with RT-DETR-L, with BoT-SORT giving the fewest ID switches ([arXiv 2604.02032](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.02032)). I found no published MOTA/IDF1 for a factory floor with forklifts; **[est.] plan for IDF1 55–70 per camera** with identical uniforms and machine occlusion, and design the IE extraction to be robust to ID fragments (trip = zone-to-zone segment, not whole-day identity).

**SAM2-family trackers** (SAMURAI, DAM4SAM, SAM2Long) hold identity through heavy overlap via mask memory but need re-initialisation for new objects and are far slower; a detector-anchored SAM2 gave the best HOTA (0.512) / IDF1 (0.614) on a soccer benchmark while classical trackers "retain substantially higher throughput" ([arXiv 2507.07603](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07603v2); [SAMURAI](https://github.com/yangchris11/samurai); [Roboflow forum comparison](https://discuss.roboflow.com/t/best-tracker-to-pair-with-rf-detr-seg-for-dense-scenes/11719)). Use them only to repair specific occlusion gaps for the single forklift, not for 14 days of 3-camera footage.

### Detector fine-tuning for carts/forklifts
- **LOCO** (TUM) is the open logistics dataset: 5,593 images, 151,428 instances of pallets, small load carriers, stillages, **forklifts and pallet trucks** ([TUM announcement](https://www.mec.ed.tum.de/en/fml/news/article/we-have-just-released-the-first-scene-understanding-dataset-for-logistics/)). YOLOv4 trained on it detected pallets well but **mostly missed forklifts/pallet trucks** due to few annotations ([arXiv 2209.13499](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.13499)); a YOLOv4-tiny follow-up reports forklift AP ≈ 53 % ([Sciforum](https://sciforum.net/paper/17882)). So: start from COCO weights (person is already strong), add LOCO, then **your own frames**.
- Data size: Roboflow's guide says a COCO-pretrained YOLO can be fine-tuned on **50–100 labelled images** for a new class ([Roboflow](https://blog.roboflow.com/yolo-training/)); Ultralytics' "best results" target is ≥1,500 images / ≥10,000 instances per class ([Ultralytics tips](https://docs.ultralytics.com/yolov5/tips_for_best_training_results)). **[est.] for 3 fixed cameras, 300–500 labelled frames per camera (≈1,500 total) covering the hand-cart types and the one forklift, sampled across lighting/time, gives per-class AP50 > 0.9** because the viewpoints are fixed; the hand carts (low, often loaded with panels) are the hard class, not the forklift.
- Alternatively tag carts with **ArUco markers**: detection needs the marker to be ≥20×20 px, ideally 30×30 px ([Find-GCP](https://github.com/zsiki/Find-GCP)); **[est.]** at 80 px/m (20 m from a 4K camera) a 40 cm marker is 32 px — workable on cart tops seen from a 5–6 m oblique camera, and it solves cart identity (§6).

### Pipelines
- **supervision + trackers (Roboflow)**: `ByteTrackTracker(track_activation_threshold=0.25, minimum_consecutive_frames=1)`, `PolygonZone` for dwell/zone entry, `ViewTransformer` for homography, with worked tutorials on dwell time and speed estimation ([supervision README](https://github.com/roboflow/supervision); [track_objects guide](https://github.com/roboflow/supervision/blob/develop/docs/how_to/track_objects.md)).
- **Ultralytics YOLO `.track()`** now ships BoT-SORT, ByteTrack, OC-SORT, Deep OC-SORT (with `with_reid`, `gmc_method`) ([track docs](https://docs.ultralytics.com/modes/track/)), plus heatmap/speed/distance guides.
- **boxmot**: one CLI for 10+ trackers with TrackEval HOTA/CLEAR metrics and a `--fps 2 --calibrate-kf` option to re-fit Kalman noise when you subsample frames ([evaluation guide](https://github.com/mikel-brostrom/boxmot/blob/master/docs/guides/evaluation.md)) — relevant if you process at 5 fps.
- **Multi-camera**: NVIDIA DeepStream MV3DT (calibrated networks, BEV fusion), OpenPTrack (RGB-D, checkerboard + ground-plane calibration; its pose module has an OpenPose licence restriction) ([OpenPTrack](https://github.com/OpenPTrack/open_ptrack_v2)), and the AI City multi-camera baselines. I could not confirm a Roboflow repo named `multicam-3d-tracking` (404 on GitHub raw); a calibration-free alternative using VGGT reached HOTA 53.1 on AI City 2024 ([arXiv 2607.22731](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.22731.md)).
- **Cross-camera re-ID with identical uniforms is unreliable**; rely on **ground-plane proximity + time in overlap zones** (hand-off when two cameras' floor estimates agree within ~1 m for ≥1 s **[est.]**), and keep ≥3–4 m of floor overlap between adjacent cameras for this.

### Role classification (operator vs handler)
Compute per-track-ID (or per-day-cluster) the **fraction of time in workstation zones vs aisle/storage zones** and the number of cart-attached trips; a simple rule (≥60 % dwell in one machine zone → operator; ≥X cart trips/h → handler **[est.]**) is what the automated-spaghetti paper effectively produces as its "time per worker per workplace" matrix ([Procedia CIRP 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212827125009813)). Cart attachment = cart box centre within ~1 m of a person's floor point and moving at the same velocity for ≥2 s **[est.]**.

## 4. Deriving IE data from tracks

- **OD matrix**: draw zones (machines, racks, hardware store, office, loading dock) on the floor plan; segment each track into *stays* and *moves*; a trip = (origin zone, destination zone, start, end, carrier type, loaded flag). Stay-point detection: standard approach is distance-radius + minimum duration; thresholds must match sensor noise ([stay-point extrema paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.06276); [US patent discussion of threshold trade-offs](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11418915)). **[est.]** for 0.3–0.5 m positional noise use radius 1.0–1.5 m and ≥8–10 s to avoid false stops in aisles. The Procedia CIRP 2025 machine-vision spaghetti paper builds exactly a **workplace-transition matrix with frequency and mean transition time** plus a worker×workplace time matrix from timestamps, with privacy-by-design ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212827125009813)). The Napier/Strathclyde UWB digital-twin work likewise classifies points as **dwell vs transit** before feeding Process Simulate ([MATEC 2024](https://www.matec-conferences.org/articles/matecconf/abs/2024/13/matecconf_icmr2024_08001/matecconf_icmr2024_08001.html)).
- **Trip purpose / loaded vs empty**: no factory paper found; practical proxies are (i) cart co-travel (§3), (ii) a small "loaded/empty cart" image classifier on the cart crop (two classes, a few hundred crops **[est.]**), (iii) destination zone type (hardware store, tool crib, office), (iv) hands-visible pose heuristic (both wrists below hip and close together → carrying) — validate against manual coding of ~200 trips.
- **Walking speeds (literature)**: free pedestrian speed **1.34 m/s, SD 0.26 m/s, normal** (Weidmann 1993, cited in [arXiv 2601.02526](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02526) and the [Vadere defaults issue](https://gitlab.lrz.de/vadere/vadere/-/issues/85)); Daamen gives 1.34 ± 0.37 ([TRB free-speed paper](https://www.pedbikeinfo.org/cms/downloads/Free%20Speed%20Distributions%20for%20Pedestrian%20Traffic.pdf)); Bohannon & Andrews' meta-analysis (23,111 subjects) gives 1.36–1.43 m/s for men 20–49 ([CSP](https://bacpar.csp.org.uk/node/338279)). **With load/cart**: couriers with trolleys moved at **0.66–1.15 m/s** ([Sci. Rep. 2023](https://doaj.org/article/0988bce6bb63489eaa7bf7f33ebd1f2f)); harvest workers carrying full trays 0.49–1.02 m/s ([arXiv 2111.09959](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09959)); hand-carried load 1.02 m/s vs shoulder 0.78 m/s ([Northumbria](https://nrl.northumbria.ac.uk/50236/1/Effects_of_load_carrying_techniques_on_gait_parameters.pdf)); pushing a moving cart is biomechanically *easier* than walking at 1.2 m/s ([Penn State](https://etda.libraries.psu.edu/catalog/9019s2443)). **[est.] priors for the twin: unloaded 1.2–1.4 m/s; pushing loaded hand cart with panels 0.8–1.1 m/s; forklift indoors 1.4–2.8 m/s (5–10 km/h; site caps 3–6 km/h near pedestrians, 6–10 km/h on open floor — [Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/setting-up-forklift-speed-limiters-by-warehouse-zone-practical-guide/), [BigRentz](https://www.bigrentz.com/blog/forklift-speed-limits-osha-rules-and-best-practices-for-safe-operation))**. Fit per-class log-normal speeds from your own tracks and replace the priors. I could not verify the MTM "5.3 TMU/ft" walking constant; a secondary source quotes `TMU = 0.3·ft + 5` ([IMD blog](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/applying-mtm-and-most-standards-in-high-variability-production)) — treat as unverified; measured speeds are better anyway.
- **Dwell, spaghetti, heatmaps**: per-zone dwell = sum of stay durations; spaghetti distance per worker-day = Σ|Δp| after smoothing (unsmoothed jitter inflates distance by tens of percent **[est.]**); heatmaps = occupancy per 0.5 m cell per hour (Ultralytics and supervision have heatmap helpers).
- **Pedestrian–forklift conflicts**: no peer-reviewed TTC/PET study for forklifts surfaced; a 2026 EECSS paper does YOLOv8 near-miss detection and criticises centroid-distance proximity because it ignores object geometry ([ICAIIS_122](https://www.avestia.com/EECSS2026_Proceedings/files/paper/ICAIIS/ICAIIS_122.pdf)). Adopt the traffic surrogate-safety approach (Ismail/Sayed/Saunier, cited in [arXiv 2008.00549](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.00549)): compute **PET** (time gap between forklift and person occupying the same 1 m cell) and **TTC** on the floor plane; **[est.] flag PET < 1.5 s or TTC < 2 s with min distance < 2 m** as a conflict, and bin by aisle intersection. Vendor numbers (94–98 % near-miss reductions) are marketing ([Voxel](https://www.voxelai.com/industry-insights/pedestrian-forklift-collision-statistics)).
- **Work sampling from video**: a 12-week trial in a discrete-manufacturing plant reports **93.4 % activity-classification accuracy (macro-F1 0.91) vs parallel manual work sampling** ([JST article](https://journals.ust.edu/index.php/JST/article/view/4068), peer-review status unclear); single-worker cell CNN/R-CNN approaches reach 94 %/73 % ([PMC7248754](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7248754/)); segmentation pre-training on factory video raised work-detection macro-F1 by 0.29 ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0267457)). For the four-state scheme a **track-derived rule** works: walking (v > 0.4 m/s), handling (v < 0.4, cart/forklift within 1.5 m or in storage zone), processing (stationary inside machine zone), waiting (stationary elsewhere, >30 s) **[est.]**; validate with manual sampling sized by `n = z²·p(1−p)/e²` → **n ≈ 323 for p = 0.3, ±5 %; 896 for ±3 %** (standard formula, e.g. [Vaia](https://vaia.com/en-us/explanations/medicine/public-health/sample-size-determination)).

## 5. Modelling movement in the discrete-event twin

| Level | What it captures | When it is enough |
|---|---|---|
| (a) **Aisle graph, t = d/v** (you have this) | travel time, distance, OD-driven workload | Relayouts whose effect is mainly *distance* and *who walks*; validated speeds + OD matrix are the key inputs |
| (b) Agent-based pedestrian (social force / AnyLogic Pedestrian Library: continuous space, obstacle and person avoidance, density-dependent speed) ([AnyLogic 9 docs](https://anylogic.help/9/libraries/pedestrian/index.html)) | congestion, counter-flow in narrow aisles, evacuation | Only if aisles are <2 m, >~10 people share them, or you must size aisle widths; the library is designed for stations/airports and its parameters are "questionable" without calibration ([arXiv 1510.07126](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.07126)) |
| (c) **Worker-as-resource with travel** — FlexSim Task Sequences (Travel on an A* / network with distance/speed) ([FlexSim Travel docs](https://docs.flexsim.com/en/22.2/Reference/ProcessFlowObjects/TaskSequences/Travel/Travel.html)); Plant Simulation **WorkerPool → FootPath → Workplace** with worker speed/efficiency attributes ([TU Darmstadt course](https://www.iim.maschinenbau.tu-darmstadt.de/kursunterlagen/tecnomatix_plant_simulation_11/worker_workplace_worker_pool_and_foot_path.html)); a pool-only model ignores walking time entirely ([FlexSim forum](https://archive.flexsim.com/threads/thread1406.html)) | staffing changes, task assignment, shared workers | This is the right level for *staffing* questions; CSIRO notes Plant Simulation worker movement is deterministic (shortest/predefined path) and proposed stochastic trajectory generation ([Schutt et al., MODSIM 2023](https://www.mssanz.org.au/modsim2023/files/schutt.pdf); Poisson/exponential inter-move models in [Tartaglia & Baumgartner](https://www.mssanz.org.au/modsim2023/files/tartaglia.pdf); survey [arXiv 2210.01344](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.01344)) |
| (d) Material-handling with blocking (one-lane aisles, deadlock) | forklift waiting, aisle capacity | Needed when the forklift and carts share one-lane aisles: FlexSim study of forklifts in narrow one-way aisles cut blocked time >30 % by relayout ([El Baz et al. 2025 via Christ Univ. archive](https://archives.christuniversity.in/items/show/26171)); DES forklift allocation with aisle constraints ([Becerra-Fernandez 2024](https://pure.urosario.edu.co/en/publications/assignment-simulation-model-for-forklifts-in-a-distribution-cente/)); blocking protocols ([Krishnamurthy et al., IMHRC 2010](https://digitalcommons.georgiasouthern.edu/pmhr_2010/17)); deadlock handling via DES+ML ([WSC 2022](https://www.informs-sim.org/wsc22papers/145.pdf)). Model each aisle segment as a capacity-1 (one-lane) or capacity-2 resource seized for `length/v`. |
| (e) **Search time for unlabelled parts** | time to locate a part in a rack | No warehouse model found; cognitive-science visual search gives search time ∝ number of similar items (conjunction search ≈ 29 ms/item in the lab, [arXiv 2310.10061](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.10061); structured layouts reduce search time, [arXiv 2409.08967](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08967)). **[est.] Practical model: search ~ lognormal with mean = a + b·(items in rack)/(labelling quality), fit a,b from measured dwell at the rack (dwell − handling time); relabelling scenarios shrink b.** |

**Which fidelity for a relayout?** No head-to-head published comparison for factories surfaced; the available evidence is that DES-with-walking (c) + validated OD/speeds (a) is standard for layout and staffing (Siemens used it for worker-proximity/partition studies — [Tecnomatix blog](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/tecnomatix/how-to-leverage-discrete-event-simulation-for-social-distancing/)), and that feeding *real* UWB trajectories into a twin changed completion-time estimates by **11 % vs plain DES** ([Kasarapu et al. 2024](https://napier-repository.worktribe.com/output/3632728/safer-and-efficient-assemblies-harnessing-real-time-worker-movements-with-digital-twins)). **Recommendation: keep (a)+(c), add (d) for the forklift/cart aisles, and trigger (b) only if measured aisle occupancy exceeds ~0.3 persons/m² or counter-flow waiting shows up in the conflict table.** Use the measured conflict and waiting times (§4) as the validation target that decides whether (b) is needed.

## 7. Korean worker-privacy practice (근로자 개인정보 보호)

- **Legal frame**: a production hall is a 비공개 장소 (restricted-access workplace), so 개인정보 보호법 §25 (public-place CCTV) does not apply but the footage is 개인정보 under §15 — 개인정보위 advises obtaining the consent of the data subjects within the camera range ([privacy.go.kr 2024.12 안내서](https://www.privacy.go.kr/front/bbs/bbsView.do?bbsNo=BBSMSTR_000000000049&bbscttNo=20779); [worklaw](https://www.worklaw.co.kr/main2022/view/view.asp?bi_pidx=36847)). The integrated 「고정형 영상정보처리기기 설치·운영 안내서(공공·민간 통합본, 2024.12)」 is on [data.go.kr](https://www.data.go.kr/data/15142230/fileData.do) and [KISA](https://www.kisa.or.kr/2060301/form?postSeq=38&lang_type=KO); the separate 2024.10 「이동형 영상정보처리기기 안내서」 addresses AI use of video ([Kim & Chang](https://www.kimchang.com/ko/insights/detail.kc?sch_section=4&idx=30477)). I could not read the full text; verify the exact clauses on workplace consent and analytics.
- **근로자참여법 제20조**: installation of 근로자 감시 설비 is a mandatory 협의사항 of the 노사협의회; courts cite the absence of 노사협의회 협의 as a factor in illegality ([Shin & Kim newsletter](https://www.shinkim.com/kor/media/newsletter/2201)). 대법원 2018도1917 (2023.6.29) overturned 업무방해 convictions of union officers who covered factory CCTV, noting the employer ran cameras against most workers' objection and had other means ([Shin & Kim](https://www.shinkim.com/kor/media/newsletter/2270)); a 2025.6.26 ruling treats *using* footage for a non-stated purpose (checking an employee's phone use) as unlawful 이용 ([lawtalk](https://www.lawtalk.co.kr/posts/115766)). PIPC has said 24-h recording of desks/screens without explicit consent is not covered by 정당한 이익 ([worklaw](https://www.worklaw.co.kr/main2022/view/view.asp?bi_pidx=36847)).
- **Consent / notice text should state** (synthesised from §15 and the guides): 수집 목적 (레이아웃·동선 분석 연구, *not* 근태·징계), 촬영 장소·대수·시간, 수집 항목 (영상, 궤적 좌표), 처리 방법 (AI 추적 후 익명 궤적만 보존), 보관 기간 (e.g. 원본 영상 분석 완료 후 즉시 파기, 최대 30일; 익명 궤적 데이터는 연구 종료 후 N년), 제3자 제공 없음, 열람·삭제 요구권, 동의 거부권 및 불이익 없음, 책임자 연락처. Practice retention in operating policies varies (20–24 days in examples found; the "30-day" figure was not confirmed — [search result](https://www.privacy.go.kr/)). Put the same content on 안내판 at hall entrances.
- **Anonymisation**: process to **pose/foot-point tracks and silhouettes**, discard raw frames after processing, never store face crops, assign random daily IDs (no cross-day identity), aggregate per-role not per-person in reports. This matches the privacy-by-design approach of the automated spaghetti paper ([Procedia CIRP 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212827125009813)). Engage the 노동조합 (if any) and 노사협의회 before installation, with a written minute; offer opt-out (worker excluded from analysis by uniform marker or by zone masking).

## 8. Concrete protocol — 60 × 36 m hall (2,160 m²) with 3 cameras

**Placement [est.]** (ceiling 6–8 m, cameras at 5.5–6 m, 4K, 2.8 mm or 4 mm):
1. **Cam A** on the long-side wall at x = 0, y = 18, tilt ≈ 30°, looking down the central aisle: covers the 12 m-wide spine for ~5–35 m.
2. **Cam B** on the opposite wall at x = 60, y = 18, same tilt, looking back: overlaps Cam A by ≥ 6 m in the hall centre (hand-off zone, shared control points).
3. **Cam C** above the dock/hardware-store/office cluster (the highest-traffic OD endpoints), tilt 40–45°, covering ~15×20 m including the forklift path.
Place no camera so that a 2–3 m machine sits between it and an aisle it must see; move the camera rather than raising it. Use the one site day for a **coverage walk** with a phone showing each RTSP stream.

**Calibration (site day, ~3 h)**
1. Chessboard (A1, 9×6) intrinsics per camera (≥25 views), `cv::fisheye` if ≥100°.
2. **8–12 floor control points per camera**: tape crosses on the floor, measure with a laser distance meter from two fixed wall references (triangulate) or from the drawn floor plan (verify plan against two laser-measured diagonals; drawings are often 2–5 % off **[est.]**). Choose points spanning near, mid and far field and both image sides; include ≥3 in each overlap zone.
3. Solve H (undistorted pixels → floor metres) with RANSAC; require hold-out RMS < 0.15 m near, < 0.4 m far.
4. **Validation walk**: one person walks a taped L-shaped path (e.g. 30 m + 15 m) at normal and cart-pushing speed, and the forklift drives a known loop; compare tracked floor path vs tape (report P50/P90 lateral error per distance band), tracked distance vs tape length, and tracked speed vs stopwatch. Repeat weekly if a camera may have been knocked.

**Processing pipeline (3 cams × 14 days)**
1. Record 1080p/4K H.265 24/7 to NVR (0.4–1.1 TB). Keep only shift hours.
2. Decode at **5 fps** (boxmot `--fps 5 --calibrate-kf` to re-fit Kalman noise). Frames: 3 × 14 d × 10 h × 3600 × 5 ≈ **7.6 M frames**.
3. Detector: YOLO-m fine-tuned on person/cart/forklift (+pose model for ankles on persons). At ~100 fps end-to-end on an RTX 4070-Ti/4090 class GPU (YOLOv8m TensorRT ≈ 540 fps detector-only on a 4070 Ti, [Stereolabs](https://www.stereolabs.com/en-be/blog/performance-of-yolo-v5-v7-and-v8); ~14 ms/frame PyTorch on a 4090, [ReadyTensor](https://app.readytensor.ai/publications/object-tracking-using-detectionsegmentationpose-estimation-yolov8-models-V26sAWYmgCe0)) that is **≈21 GPU-hours [est.]**, ~70 h at 30 fps; one workstation GPU over a weekend.
4. Tracker: BoT-SORT (ReID on, `gmc` off for fixed cameras) per camera; ground-plane fusion in overlap zones; RTS smoothing.
5. Zone engine (supervision `PolygonZone` or your own): stays/moves, OD, dwell, state labels, conflicts.
6. Manual QA: 2 h of footage coded by hand for ID switches, trips and states (work-sampling n ≈ 300–900).

**Output tables and twin fields**

- `trips.csv`: `trip_id, date, cam_set, track_id, role, carrier (walk|handcart|forklift), loaded (0/1/unk), origin_zone, dest_zone, t_start, t_end, path_len_m, mean_speed_mps, n_stops_en_route` → **DES**: OD-matrix arrival rates per hour (`λ_od`), speed distributions per carrier/loaded class (replace t = d/v constants), transport batch/empty-return ratio, trip generation per product order.
- `dwell.csv`: `zone_id, date, track_id, role, t_in, t_out, duration_s, state (processing|handling|waiting|search)` → processing-time distributions per machine, search-time distribution at racks (§5e), waiting at shared resources, operator vs handler staffing shares, machine utilisation cross-check.
- `conflicts.csv`: `t, cell_x, cell_y, aisle_id, agent_a, agent_b, min_dist_m, PET_s, TTC_s, severity` → aisle capacity (one-lane segments), forklift priority rules, validation targets for the material-handling layer (d), and the trigger for pedestrian-level modelling (b).
- Plus `od_matrix_hourly.csv`, `speed_dist.csv` (per class: mean, SD, lognormal params), `heatmap_*.png`, and `validation.csv` (walk-test errors, ID-switch rate, work-sampling agreement).

**Key uncertainties to close on the site day**: machine heights vs camera sightlines (the leg-occlusion bias in §2 dominates everything), whether carts can be tagged (solves cart identity cheaply), and the 노사협의회/consent sign-off — without it, record nothing.
